## Rules

1. **Write everything a person reads in Uzbek (latin script).** Labels,
   reasons, ad copy, form questions. The only English is the `query` field
   of an interest, which must be Meta's own catalogue name for it.

2. **Interest queries must be terms Meta's catalogue plausibly contains.**
   Common, broad English nouns: "Restaurants", "Barbershop", "Physical
   fitness", "Real estate". Not invented phrases, not brand names of local
   businesses, not sentences. Every query is searched for verbatim against
   Meta, and one that does not exist is dropped.

   A name from the confirmed list above is known to exist. Copy its `query`
   exactly as written there - a rephrasing is a new word and is searched
   for like one.

3. **Mix broad and narrow interests.** With a small daily budget, an
   audience built only from narrow interests will not deliver. Mark 4 to 6
   as `recommended` - the ones you would actually run today.

   Eight to ten interests, not twenty. The owner ticks boxes on a phone,
   and a list they scroll past is a list they accept without reading.

4. **The audience follows the offer, not the category.** A tasting menu
   restaurant and a lunch canteen are the same category and different
   audiences. Say which one you concluded from, in the audience's `reason`.

   Reasons are short. The audience gets one sentence; an interest gets
   three or four words, because it sits under a checkbox and is read at a
   glance or not at all. Writing a paragraph there is the single most
   expensive habit in this answer and nothing on the screen shows it.

5. **`radius_km` follows how far people travel for this.** Nobody crosses
   a city for coffee; they do for a clinic.

6. **Leave `genders` empty unless the product is genuinely for one.**
   Halving the audience on a guess doubles the price of every result.

7. **Write exactly {{ creative_count }} adverts in `creatives` - one per picture listed
   above, in that order.** A picture that is an Instagram post is not on the
   list: it goes with the caption its owner already published, and nothing
   here proposes words for it. When the list says there are none, return
   `creatives` as an empty list.

   Each advert is written from what the owner said about its picture. When
   they wrote one text for all of them, the pictures are one offer seen
   several ways, and the only thing worth varying is the angle. Take them in
   this order and stop when you have enough:

   - the first opens on the **problem** the customer already has;
   - the second opens on the **result** they get afterwards;
   - the third opens on the **offer** itself - the price, the thing, the
     appointment;
   - any after that takes whichever of the three fits its picture best,
     without repeating a sentence already written.

   Each has its own `headline` and its own `description` - the extra line
   under the headline. Two versions of one sentence with the words swapped
   is the one outcome that wastes a slot: the owner cannot tell them apart
   on the screen, and neither can the numbers a month later.

8. **Ad copy: no emoji, no exclamation marks, no "the best in town".** One
   concrete benefit, then what to do next. Uzbek that sounds like a person
   wrote it, not a translation. Two or three short sentences in
   `primary_text` - a phone cuts the text off after about a hundred
   characters and hides the rest behind "See more", so a fourth sentence is
   written for nobody. Never promise anything the owner did not say the
   business does.

9. **`form_questions`: the fewest that let them follow up.** Two or three.
   Name and phone are almost always enough, and every extra question
   costs leads.

10. Never mention Meta, targeting, objectives, CPL, or any advertising term
    in text the owner reads. They run a business; the machinery is our
    problem.
