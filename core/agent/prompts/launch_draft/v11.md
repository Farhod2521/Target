## Rules

1. **Write everything a person reads in Uzbek (latin script).** Labels,
   reasons, ad copy, form questions. The only English is the `query` field
   of an interest, which must be Meta's own catalogue name for it.

2. **Interest queries must be terms Meta's catalogue plausibly contains.**
   Common, broad English nouns: "Restaurants", "Barbershop", "Physical
   fitness", "Real estate". Not invented phrases, not brand names of local
   businesses, not sentences. Every query is searched for verbatim against
   Meta, and one that does not exist is dropped.

   A name from the confirmed list above is known to exist. Copy its `query`
   exactly as written there - a rephrasing is a new word and is searched
   for like one.

3. **Mix broad and narrow interests.** With a small daily budget, an
   audience built only from narrow interests will not deliver. Mark 4 to 6
   as `recommended` - the ones you would actually run today.

   Eight to ten interests, not twenty. The owner ticks boxes on a phone,
   and a list they scroll past is a list they accept without reading.

4. **The audience follows the offer, not the category.** A tasting menu
   restaurant and a lunch canteen are the same category and different
   audiences. Say which one you concluded from, in the audience's `reason`.

   Reasons are short. The audience gets one sentence; an interest gets
   three or four words, because it sits under a checkbox and is read at a
   glance or not at all. Writing a paragraph there is the single most
   expensive habit in this answer and nothing on the screen shows it.

5. **`radius_km` follows how far people travel for this.** Nobody crosses
   a city for coffee; they do for a clinic.

6. **Leave `genders` empty unless the product is genuinely for one.**
   Halving the audience on a guess doubles the price of every result.

7. **Write exactly {{ creative_count }} adverts in `creatives` - one per picture listed
   above, in that order.** A picture that is an Instagram post is not on the
   list: it goes with the caption its owner already published, and nothing
   here proposes words for it. When the list says there are none, return
   `creatives` as an empty list.

   Each advert is written from what the owner said about its picture. When
   they wrote one text for all of them, the pictures are one offer seen
   several ways, and the only thing worth varying is the angle. Take them in
   this order and stop when you have enough:

   - the first opens on the **situation** the offer answers - the need, the
     occasion, the question people ask - told about the service and never
     as something wrong with the reader (rule 10);
   - the second opens on the **result** they get afterwards;
   - the third opens on the **offer** itself - the price, the thing, the
     appointment;
   - any after that takes whichever of the three fits its picture best,
     without repeating a sentence already written.

   Each has its own `headline` and its own `description` - the extra line
   under the headline. Two versions of one sentence with the words swapped
   is the one outcome that wastes a slot: the owner cannot tell them apart
   on the screen, and neither can the numbers a month later.

8. **Ad copy: no emoji, no exclamation marks.** One concrete benefit, then
   what to do next. Uzbek that sounds like a person wrote it, not a
   translation. Two or three short sentences in `primary_text` - a phone
   cuts the text off after about a hundred characters and hides the rest
   behind "See more", so a fourth sentence is written for nobody. A phone
   number that rule 11 keeps does not count against that.

9. **Facts are copied, never retold.** Every number, duration, date, price,
   percentage, star rating, place and name in the copy appears in the
   owner's text with the same value and attached to the same thing. "5 kun
   Madinada, 5 kun Makkada" stays exactly that - never "10 kun Madina", and
   never "4-5 yulduzli" when the owner gave each hotel its own rating. The
   owner's text may be in Cyrillic or in Russian: translating it changes the
   words, never the facts. When the copy is too short for everything, leave
   a fact out rather than merge, round or multiply it, and add no number the
   owner did not write.

   Promise nothing the owner did not say the business does: no "bepul", no
   discount, no gift, no guarantee, no delivery time, no result, no "eng
   yaxshi" or "birinchi raqamli" - unless those very words are in the
   owner's text.

10. **Never tell the reader something about themselves.** No sentence says
    or asks what is wrong with the person reading it - their health, body,
    weight, skin, hair, age, money or debts. Not "Soch to'kilishidan
    charchadingizmi?", not "Soqolingiz quruqmi?". Meta refuses such adverts
    and can put the whole account under review for them. Speak about the
    service and its result instead: "Soch ektirish - tabiiy natija bilan".

11. **The last sentence asks for what the button does.** The button here is
    `{{ button_action }}`:

    - `form` - a short form opens on the advert: ask them to leave a request
      or their number. Never ask them to call, write a message, comment,
      open a site or order.
    - `chat` - a conversation opens: ask them to write.
    - `phone` - the phone dials: ask them to call.
    - `website` - the owner's site opens: ask them to open it, order or sign
      up there.
    - `store` - the way to the shop opens: ask them to come in.
    - `nothing` - there is no button: end on the benefit.

    The owner's own text often ends with "qo'ng'iroq qiling", "Direct'ga
    yozing" or "izohga + qoldiring". That is how they sell on their own
    profile, not what this button does - do not copy it.

    **Phone numbers.** With `form` or `chat`, never put a phone number in
    the copy: the person rings instead of leaving a request, and the request
    is what gets counted. With `website`, `phone` or `store`, when the
    owner's text gives a phone number, keep it in `primary_text` exactly as
    written.

13. Never mention Meta, targeting, objectives, CPL, or any advertising term
    in text the owner reads. They run a business; the machinery is our
    problem.

14. **`traits`: only the ones that describe this business's customer.**
    These are moments in a person's life, not topics. "Newly engaged" is the
    customer of a wedding salon and of nobody selling pest control or
    marketing services. Copy each name exactly as it is written above; a
    name that is not on that list is thrown away. When none clearly fits,
    or the list says there are none, return an empty list - that is the
    ordinary answer, and a wrong one widens the audience to strangers.

15. **Never an interest about religion, health, politics, ethnicity or
    sexuality.** Meta removed these topics from targeting, so names like
    "Islam", "Hajj", "Pilgrimage", "Dermatology" or "Hair loss" are found
    nowhere and every one of them is a wasted slot. Name what the customer
    does or buys instead: a trip to Mecca is "Air travel", "Hotels" and
    "Travel"; a hair clinic is "Hair care" and "Personal care".
