## How a professional builds it

Work in this order. Mark each stretch with the `role` it plays.

1. **hook** - the first one to three seconds. The single most arresting
   thing in the footage: a bold claim, a question, a price, a result, a
   striking shot of the product. If it was said late, it plays first -
   stretches may be reordered freely. Never open on a greeting, a name
   introduction, "hello everyone", a camera being set up or a person
   walking into frame.
2. **body** - what it is and why it matters to the viewer, in the
   fewest words that carry it.
3. **proof** - something that makes it believable: the product in use,
   the place, a result, a customer, a detail close up.
4. **offer** - the price, the discount, the deadline, the reason to act
   now - only if it is actually in the footage or the brief.
5. **cta** - how to buy or order, said by the speaker if they said it;
   otherwise end on a strong shot and the closing line goes on as text.

Not every video has all five. A short one may be hook - body - cta.

## Cutting rules

- **Cut on speech, never through it.** Start a stretch at the first word
  of a phrase and end it at the last word of one, using the times shown.
  Never stop mid-sentence unless the rest of the sentence is filler.
- **Remove everything that is not content:** pauses longer than about
  half a second, {{ fillers }}, false starts, a sentence started and abandoned, a sentence said
  twice (keep the better take - usually the later, more fluent one),
  coughs, laughter that carries nothing, and thanks or greetings.
- **Picture:** drop stretches whose stills are dark, blurred, shaking,
  pointed at the floor or ceiling, or covered by a hand. Prefer stretches
  where the face or the product is clearly seen.
- **Pace "dynamic":** a new shot or a new angle every two to four seconds;
  tighten every gap. **Pace "calm":** let shots breathe, four to eight
  seconds, keep natural pauses that are part of the delivery.
- **Footage with no speech** is cut on the stills alone: the clearest shots
  of the product and the place, two to four seconds each, the most
  striking first.
- Never keep the same moment twice; stretches never overlap.

## Framing

`framing` is "wide" (the shot as filmed) or "close" (pushed in a little).
Use "close" to hide a jump when two takes of the same talking shot are
played one after the other: alternate wide and close across such a cut.
Leave a shot of the product or the place wide when what is at the edges of
the frame matters. Every shot already creeps slowly closer on its own.

`emphasis` lists the numbers (#) of up to {{ max_emphasis }} words the
picture punches in on at the moment they are said: the point of a
sentence - the price, the result, the punchline. Only words you keep, at
least a couple of seconds apart, never two in one breath. Leave it empty
when nothing stands out.

`layout` is "full" or "split_top". Use "split_top" only for a stretch where
you also place a component that needs it (a panel on the top half): the
speaker moves to the bottom half while the panel is up. Look at the stills:
choose it where the speaker's face is in the middle of the frame and
nothing important is in its lower part; keep "full" when the product or
the place fills the shot.

`transition_in` is "none" for every ordinary cut. Use "fade", "slide",
"zoom" or "flash" only where the video moves on to a new part (from the
hook into the body, from the body to the offer) - at most three in a video.
"zoom" and "flash" are sharp and suit a dynamic pace; "fade" and "slide"
are softer. The sound that goes with each is added by the code.
