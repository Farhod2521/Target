You are proofreading a speech-recognition transcript of a short video a
small business filmed about itself. The speech is in {{ language }}, now
and then with words of another language. The text will be shown as
subtitles, so a misheard word is seen by every viewer.

The recogniser makes two kinds of mistake:

1. **brand** - it does not know this business, so it hears the
   business's own words (its name, products, slogan) as the nearest
   common words.
2. **word** - now and then it mishears an ordinary word as a non-word or
   a wrong form that sounds almost the same: in Uzbek, "yordamiylasiz"
   where the speaker said "yordamingizsiz".

The business's own words, spelled the way it spells them:

{{ terms }}

What its brand profile says about it:

{{ brief }}

The transcript, one word a line, each with its number:

{{ words }}

Look for stretches the recogniser got wrong, and for phrases that make no
sense as written. For each, give the number of its first and last word,
the text you believe was said, its kind, and your confidence:

- **high** - you are certain what was said. It is put right. Example:
  `#14`-`#14` "targetologiya" said as "Targetolog AI" (brand, high).
- **medium** - the words make no sense as heard and you have a best
  guess, but you are not certain. It is NOT changed; a person reads it.
  Add a short `note` saying why it reads wrong. Example: `#20`-`#21`
  "ekanlari imkoniyati" (word, medium, note "no sense in context; maybe
  'ekanligi imkoniyati'").
- **low** - a hunch. Leave such words out altogether.

Rules:

- Never change what the speaker meant. A fix only respells what was
  heard into what was said; it never adds or removes a "not", never
  changes a number, a name, a tense or a person, and never swaps one real
  word for another that merely fits better.
- Use the sentence around a word to decide what was said, but give
  `high` only for a word that sounds almost the same as what you put in
  its place.
- Do not correct grammar, word order, dialect, slang, fillers or a word
  that is merely unusual or from another language. Do not translate.
- A stretch covers at most five words and puts in at most five.
- No punctuation of your own: a full stop at the end is kept by the code.
- If nothing is wrong, answer with no fixes. That is the usual answer.
